# Part I — Consolidated Official Rules

## 1. Event identity and purpose

**Build What Moves India** is a hackathon focused on improving Indian public-service websites and digital-service journeys.

It is an **independent builder initiative presented by Varun Mayya in partnership with OpenAI**. It is not an official government hackathon. Attendance or involvement by government or public-service representatives must not be treated as government endorsement.

Purpose: identify a real problem encountered on an Indian public-service website or digital service, then build a simpler, clearer, and more useful solution.

## 2. Eligibility

- Participants must be **18 years old or older**.
- Participants may enter:
  - individually; or
  - in a team of **no more than two people**.
- Each teammate must register separately with their own email address.
- Each teammate must provide the other teammate's registered email in the Project Submission form.
- Developers, designers, product thinkers, students, and independent builders may apply.
- Participants must be able to frame the selected problem and build the prototype themselves.
- Geographic residency or citizenship restrictions are **not stated** in the published materials. **[Organizer confirmation required]**
- Registration is only an expression of interest. It does not confirm selection or guarantee a place in the hackathon.

## 3. Challenge scope

Each submission must address one specific problem within an Indian public-service website or digital journey, or rethink an entire public-service experience.

Possible areas include travel, taxes, pensions, certificates, payments, grievances, and other public needs. References to IRCTC, EPFO, and the Income Tax portal are examples, not a fixed list.

The submission must:

1. solve one clearly defined user problem;
2. provide a complete citizen journey from start to finish;
3. be simpler, clearer, or more useful than the current experience;
4. be designed for real Indian users, including users on mobile devices, slower connections, or with limited digital experience;
5. use mock or synthetic data where real personal information, payments, OTPs, accounts, or government systems would otherwise be involved;
6. identify what works, what is mocked, and what depends on unavailable production systems; and
7. explain how the solution could operate safely at larger scale.

Reviewers will assess the citizen-facing experience, not an admin panel. A static design or Figma-only submission is insufficient. Every feature demonstrated must work.

## 4. Required technology and disclosure

- Codex is mandatory for the prototype submitted to this hackathon.
- Codex must be meaningfully involved in the build, not added only for submission.
- Participants must explain how Codex contributed.
- Other development tools, libraries, starter templates, and open-source components may be used if participants have the right to use them and disclose them clearly.
- If a public-service body later develops the solution, published materials impose no technology restriction on that later implementation.
- OpenAI will provide support to builds selected among the 10 finalists.

**Source inconsistency:** The Builder Brief says a prototype should be “built with Codex or powered by an OpenAI model,” while the FAQ says Codex is mandatory. This draft applies the stricter FAQ requirement. Organizers should clarify whether meaningful use of another OpenAI model without Codex qualifies.

## 5. Safety, security, privacy, and representation rules

Participants must not:

- access, test, scan, exploit, disrupt, or interfere with any live government system;
- reverse-engineer private systems;
- use undocumented private APIs;
- scrape personal, restricted, or confidential information;
- use real Aadhaar numbers, PAN details, passwords, OTPs, payment details, health information, government-login data, or other sensitive personal information;
- connect to a live government system unless organizers provide a specifically approved public sandbox;
- copy government source code or build on government infrastructure without authorization;
- present a prototype as an official government product;
- use government names, marks, or logos in a way that implies approval, partnership, or endorsement;
- submit an old project with only minor changes; or
- include code, designs, assets, data, or other material they do not own or have permission to use.

Each prototype should be clearly labelled **“Independent hackathon prototype — not an official government service.”**

## 6. Submission requirements

Initial submissions close on **28 August 2026 at 8:00 PM India Standard Time (IST)**. Published rules state there is no grace period after the form closes.

Each submission must include:

1. **Live prototype:** A public link that opens in a browser without requesting access. Reviewers will not download a mobile application.
2. **Mock credentials:** If login is required, working mock consumer credentials. Never provide a real password or sensitive data.
3. **Demo video:** One public video no longer than two minutes.
   - First minute: demonstrate the project as a citizen.
   - Second minute: explain how it was built and why key choices were made.
   - Both teammates may present.
4. **Project summary:** Fewer than 250 words explaining the project and why it improves on the current solution.
5. **Team information:** For a two-person team, each teammate must provide the other person's registered email. Solo participants should leave that field blank.
6. **Working links:** Every submitted link must work without an access request.

Participants must use the same email address throughout registration, submission, shortlisting, and resubmission. Published rules state that an entry cannot be moved to another email address.

The public website does not currently identify the Project Submission form URL. **[Organizer confirmation required]**

## 7. Judging criteria

Published criteria do not specify weights. Builds will be assessed on:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Problem | Is this a real and important user problem? |
| Working build | Does the main journey work? |
| Usability | Is the experience simpler, clearer, and more accessible? |
| Product thinking | Are choices thoughtful and well explained? |
| End-to-end thinking | Does the solution address backend, infrastructure, and process needs, not only interface design? |
| Honesty | Are limitations, mock data, and dependencies clearly disclosed? |

Visual quality matters but cannot replace a real problem, working core journey, or practical solution.

## 8. Selection process and dates

### Stage 1: All submissions to shortlist of 250

- Review period: **28 August to 1 September 2026**.
- Reviewers: the organizing team together with OpenAI.
- Outcome: top 250 builds shortlisted.
- Notification: everyone who submitted receives an email with their result.

### Stage 2: Shortlist of 250 to 10 finalists

- Shortlisted participants receive one week of mentorship through a WhatsApp group.
- Five mentors from engineering, technology, and the OpenAI team will support participants.
- Improved builds must be resubmitted in the same format by **7 September 2026**, using the same email addresses.
- Ten finalists will be announced between **8 and 12 September 2026**.
- The top 250 will be recognized on a public page.

### Finale

- Date: **12 September 2026**.
- Location: Bengaluru; exact venue and timing will be sent to selected builders.
- Finalists present live to founders, creators, mentors, and invited government officials.
- The finale will be filmed but not live-streamed.
- Winners will be announced on the same day.
- Organizers will contact finalists about technical checks, rehearsal, travel, accommodation, and filming consent.

Selection or winning does not guarantee adoption or implementation by any government body.

## 9. Prizes

As stated on the public website:

- **Top 10:** one year of Codex Pro and a Codex Micro.
- **Top 3:** a MacBook in addition to the Top 10 prizes.
- **Winner:** a trip to San Francisco, subject to visa approval, in addition to all prizes above.

Prize model, specifications, subscription terms, travel inclusions, transferability, tax treatment, delivery timing, substitutions, and treatment of team entries are not stated. **[Organizer confirmation required]**

## 10. Ownership and submission rights

- Participants retain full rights to their builds.
- Participants may submit only work they own or have permission to use.
- Third-party tools, libraries, templates, code, assets, and data must be properly licensed and disclosed.
- Registration-form applicants confirm that their team has the right to use all code, designs, and other submission material.
- Registration-form applicants allow organizers to showcase their submissions.

## 11. Communications, data, and recordings

Registration currently requests:

- full name;
- email address;
- WhatsApp number;
- age;
- solo or team status;
- profession;
- teammate email, if applicable; and
- ability to attend the Bengaluru finale.

Published statements provide that:

- registration and submission data will be managed by Varun Mayya's team;
- OpenAI will not have access to registration and submission data;
- updates will be sent through registered email addresses and WhatsApp numbers;
- email recipients may unsubscribe from updates at any time;
- registration-form applicants allow organizers to use photos or recordings of their participation in hackathon-related content; and
- finalists will receive separate information about filming consent.

The FAQ says a final privacy notice should identify who can access data, why it is collected, and when it will be deleted. No event-specific privacy-notice page was found during review. **[Organizer confirmation required]**

## 17. Intellectual property and showcase permission

Before publication, organizers should define:

# Part III — Publication Blockers and Website Findings

Resolve these before treating this document as final terms:

1. **Organizer identity missing:** No legal entity, registered address, or official contact is published.
2. **No event privacy notice:** `/privacy` was not available. FAQ itself says a final notice should explain access, purpose, and deletion.
3. **No standalone terms page:** `/terms` and `/terms-and-conditions` were not available. Google Forms links only to Google's own terms and privacy policy.
4. **Codex requirement conflict:** Builder Brief allows “Codex or an OpenAI model” but then requires meaningful Codex use; FAQ says Codex is mandatory.
5. **Eligibility incomplete:** Residency, citizenship, employee/contractor exclusions, family-member exclusions, sanctions restrictions, and prior-winner rules are not stated.
6. **Submission route missing:** Public site does not identify the Project Submission form URL.
7. **Judging mechanics missing:** No weights, tie-breaker, conflict policy, judge list, appeal process, or final-decision clause.
8. **Prize details incomplete:** Exact models, values, team allocation, tax obligations, delivery, substitutions, and travel inclusions are absent.
9. **Finale costs unclear:** Travel and accommodation arrangements are mentioned but funding and eligibility are not stated.
10. **IP licence underspecified:** Participants retain rights and allow showcasing, but licence scope, duration, media, attribution, and withdrawal are undefined.
11. **Media consent underspecified:** Registration includes broad showcase/photo/recording consent; finale consent details are promised later.
12. **Data governance incomplete:** Controller identity, retention/deletion schedule, processors, transfers, security, rights process, and WhatsApp opt-out are missing.
13. **OpenAI data-access ambiguity:** FAQ says OpenAI will not access registration or submission data, while Builder Brief says submissions are reviewed by organizer team together with OpenAI. Clarify whether OpenAI receives only public prototype/video links, anonymized judging packets, or other entry data.
14. **Enforcement rules missing:** No explicit disqualification, investigation, misconduct, plagiarism, late-link failure, or appeal process.
15. **Changes/cancellation absent:** No rule-change, postponement, force-majeure, or cancellation process.
16. **Legal framework absent:** No governing law, jurisdiction, liability terms, severability clause, waiver clause, or organizer contact.
17. **Discoverability issue:** No XML sitemap was found, and tested policy routes returned 404 pages.

## Recommended publication set

Publish and link these pages from both the website footer and every registration/submission form:

- `/rules` — final official hackathon rules;
- `/terms` — approved terms and conditions;
- `/privacy` — event-specific privacy notice;
- `/code-of-conduct` — participant and event conduct;
- `/accessibility` — accommodations and contact route; and
- `/contact` — organizer identity and support/grievance details.

Add version number, publication date, and change log to each policy page. Obtain fresh consent if a later change materially affects participant rights, data use, eligibility, judging, or prizes.
